### AC) Are Touq's tablet signatures good enough, and what would make them stronger?

Background in plain words: Saudi Arabia's **Electronic Transactions Law** (Royal Decree M/18, 2007) says an electronic signature has the same effect as a handwritten one if the signer approved the content and the signature is really theirs. Consent to sign electronically can be implicit (by doing it). Exclusions are personal-status matters and (per some sources) real-estate deeds; vet consent forms are not excluded. A party relying on the signature is expected to be able to check it. In practice, what helps a clinic in a dispute is **evidence**: who signed, when, which exact text, and proof nothing changed afterwards (an "audit trail").

| Option | What it does | Price (seen 2026-11-05) | Saudi fit | Effort with React + edge functions | Risk / lock-in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What Touq has + a tamper fingerprint** | Keep the finger signature. Add a database trigger that stores `sha256(body ‖ signer_name ‖ signature ‖ signed_at)` and the staff member's id; print the hash short-form on the PDF | Free | Fine for routine vet consent (surgery, hospital stay, euthanasia) | **S**: one migration (`pgcrypto` `digest()`), one line on the print | None. Doesn't prove identity of the owner, only that the record is unchanged |
| **+ owner phone OTP** | Send a 4-digit code by SMS/WhatsApp to the owner's phone on file before they sign; store "verified phone" with the waiver | Per message, see topic B (Unifonic/Taqnyat) | Strong practical identity proof; owners are used to OTPs | M: edge function + provider from topic B | Message cost; needs the owner's own phone at the desk |
| **Signit** (Saudi) | E-signature SaaS; email, SMS, **Absher/Nafath** identity check; says it is certified by the Digital Government Authority | From ~SAR 80/user/month (vendor page); **API and Nafath only on higher tiers**, price on request | Built for KSA, Arabic, local data storage (vendor claim) | M–L: send PDF via API, owner signs in their browser, webhook back | Monthly cost per clinic; owner must leave Touq's flow |
| **Zoho Sign** | E-signature with **emdha** signing via the Nafath app | Paid plans; Nafath/emdha pricing not public | Good for KSA | M–L | Same as above |
| **DocuSign** | Global leader | ~$25–40/user/month (from a competitor's comparison, unverified) | No Nafath (per a competitor, unverified); data outside KSA | M–L | Most expensive; PDPL transfer |

For Touq specifically: a waiver is signed **in the clinic, in front of staff, on the clinic's tablet**, which is already good evidence. Nafath-level signing matters for contracts with strangers at a distance, not for a pet's surgery consent. The cheap gap is proving the stored record was not changed.

**Recommendation (AC):** now: add the **tamper fingerprint** (S) and print it on the waiver PDF. At v3: if partner clinics ask, add **owner phone OTP** using the SMS provider chosen in topic B. Skip Signit/DocuSign/Zoho for waivers; revisit only if Touq ever signs **clinic-to-Touq subscription contracts** (Signit fits that, because of Nafath).

### AD) Standard diagnosis lists (so reports can count "how many skin cases this month")

Background: today `medical_records.diagnosis` is free text. That's fast to type, but nobody can ask "how many parvovirus cases in October?" or spot outbreaks, and Arabic/English spelling varies. Vet software abroad offers a **pick-list of standard diagnosis terms**, each with a code, while still allowing free text.

| Option | What it is | Price / licence (seen 2026-11-05) | Arabic | Effort | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VeNom codes** | UK list of diagnoses, presenting complaints and procedures, maintained by the RVC-led VeNom Coding Group; widely used by UK practice software and research (e.g. VetCompass) | Given to practices and software makers **on request** (licence terms not found online; site not reachable from here) | **English only**; Arabic labels would be Touq's own work | M: import a CSV into a `diagnosis_terms` table + a search-as-you-type picker | Need permission; group controls changes |
| **AAHA Diagnostic Terms** | US list, mapped to SNOMED CT | **Royalty-free** licence, condition: keep the SNOMED mapping | English only | M | Must keep the mapping intact |
| **SNOMED CT Veterinary Extension (VetSCT)** | The vet part of the big international medical terminology (Virginia Tech) | Follows SNOMED licensing. **Saudi Arabia is a SNOMED member since 2019** (via NHIC, the National Health Information Center), which normally makes use inside the Kingdom free; confirm with NHIC | No Arabic veterinary translation found | L (very large, complex) | Overkill for v3 |
| **Clinic's own short list** (no service) | Each clinic keeps "top 50 diagnoses" in EN + AR, like services/prices today | Free | ✅ native Arabic | **S** | Not comparable across clinics |

**Recommendation (AD):** now: nothing. At v3: start with the **clinic's own short bilingual list** (an optional pick-list above the free-text box, S). If Touq later wants cross-clinic statistics, email the VeNom group for a software licence (best fit for small-animal practice) and map each clinic's list to VeNom codes. Skip VetSCT.
